Buying a home in Texas is exciting — and insurance is one of the items that can stall a closing if you wait too long. Lenders require a homeowners policy (or an equivalent) before they will fund, and the right policy is more than a checkbox on your closing checklist.

## Start quotes before you are under contract

Request quotes as soon as you are seriously shopping, not the week of closing. Carriers need time to inspect the property, review claims history, and confirm roof age, updates, and construction type. In competitive Austin and Central Texas markets, a last-minute surprise (an uninsurable roof, a prior claim, or a high deductible you did not expect) is far easier to solve with two or three weeks of runway.

If you are working with a Spyglass Realty agent, tell your insurance advisor as soon as you write an offer. Option periods in Texas are short; insurance questions belong in that window alongside inspections.

## What you will be asked for

Have this information ready so quotes come back faster and more accurately:

- Property address and year built
- Square footage, construction type, and number of stories
- Roof age, material, and any recent replacement
- Updates to electrical, plumbing, and HVAC
- Current (or prior) carrier and claims history
- Desired dwelling limit, deductibles, and extra coverages (jewelry, water backup, extended replacement cost)
- Mortgage lender name and loan number once you have them

Photos from the listing or inspection often help, especially for older roofs or unique construction.

## How insurance shows up in your contract and closing

In a typical Texas residential contract, the buyer is responsible for obtaining insurance. Your lender will require an evidence-of-insurance (or binder) that lists them as mortgagee, with coverage equal to at least the loan amount and usually matching replacement cost for the dwelling.

Closing cannot fund without that document. Build in time for:

1. Quote comparison and carrier selection
2. Any property inspection the insurer requires
3. Binding coverage effective on the closing date
4. Delivery of the binder to the lender and title company

If the closing date moves, tell your advisor immediately so the effective date and binder can be updated.

## Coverage that Texas buyers often overlook

A standard HO-3 policy covers the dwelling, other structures, personal property, loss of use, and liability — but several gaps show up often in Central Texas:

- **Wind and hail deductibles** are frequently a percentage of the dwelling limit, not a flat dollar amount.
- **Flood is not included.** See our article on flood insurance in Central Texas if the property sits near a creek, in a mapped zone, or in an area that has flooded even once.
- **Water backup and sewer backup** usually require an endorsement.
- **Replacement cost vs. actual cash value** on the roof can change a claim payout dramatically.
