If you drive in Austin, auto insurance is not optional — Texas law requires it, and a single crash on I-35 can cost far more than the state minimum covers. Whether you just moved here, bought a car, or your renewal jumped again, it pays to understand what your policy actually does and where the gaps are.

## Texas minimum coverage: what the law requires

Texas requires every driver to carry liability insurance at the **30/60/25** level:

- **$30,000** bodily injury coverage per person
- **$60,000** bodily injury coverage per accident (total, no matter how many people)
- **$25,000** property damage coverage per accident

Liability pays the *other* driver and their passengers when you are at fault. It does not pay to repair your own car or cover your own medical bills.

That minimum sounds like a lot until you price a hospital stay, an ambulance ride, and three months of physical therapy. A single night in a trauma center can eat through $30,000 quickly. If your liability limits are too low, the other driver can come after your assets for the difference.

A bill in the 2025 Texas Legislature (HB 4178) would have raised the minimums to 50/100/40, but it did not pass — it died in committee. So 30/60/25 remains the legal floor, and it has not changed in over a decade. Most agents recommend carrying well above the minimum.

## Coverage you should actually understand

Texas auto policies come with several moving parts. Here is what each one does in plain English:

**Liability** — pays others when you cause the crash. The legal minimum, but rarely the right choice.

**Collision** — pays to repair or replace *your* car after an accident, regardless of fault, minus your deductible. If your car is financed, your lender likely requires this.

**Comprehensive** — pays for damage to your car that is *not* a collision: theft, fire, vandalism, flooding, and — critically for Austin — **hail**. A hailstorm can total a car in minutes. If you have a loan, your lender usually requires comprehensive too.

**Personal Injury Protection (PIP)** — pays your medical bills, lost wages, and some non-medical costs after a crash, regardless of fault. Texas insurers must offer a minimum of $2,500 in PIP. You have to reject it *in writing* to remove it. Do not reject it without understanding what you give up — PIP pays you even when the other driver is uninsured or underinsured.

**Medical Payments (MedPay)** — similar to PIP but narrower. Pays medical bills only, not lost wages. Can stack on top of PIP for extra cushion.

**Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist (UM/UIM)** — pays you when the at-fault driver has no insurance or not enough. Texas insurers must offer this coverage. You must reject it in writing. Given how many uninsured drivers are on Texas roads, this is one of the most valuable endorsements on your policy.

**Towing and labor** — roadside assistance. Cheap and often worth it.

**Rental reimbursement** — pays for a rental car while yours is being repaired after a covered claim. If you depend on your car to get to work, add it.

## Austin-specific factors that move your rate

Auto insurance in Austin is not priced the same as in Houston, Dallas, or San Antonio. Carriers look at local data, and several things push Austin rates:

**Traffic density on I-35 and MoPac.** Austin sees roughly 11,000–12,000 reportable crashes per year, and a large share cluster along the I-35 corridor. Congestion, rear-end collisions, and high-speed merge zones all feed into the frequency and severity data carriers use to set rates by ZIP code.

**Hail and severe weather.** Central Texas hail storms are notorious. Comprehensive claims for glass and body damage spike every spring. Carriers price that risk into comprehensive premiums.

**Uninsured drivers.** Texas has one of the highest uninsured motorist rates in the country. Nationally, about 14% of drivers carry no insurance, per the Insurance Information Institute. Some estimates put the Texas figure even higher. That raises everyone's premium because carriers have to pay uninsured-motorist claims more often.

**Your driving record and claims history.** A single at-fault accident can raise your Austin premium by 30–60% at renewal. A DUI can double it. Clean records keep you in the best pricing tier.

## What Austin drivers actually pay

Rates vary widely by carrier, age, vehicle, ZIP code, and driving record. Independent analyses put the average full-coverage premium in Austin at roughly **$2,900–$3,600 per year**, though clean-record drivers in some ZIPs pay less and high-risk profiles pay substantially more.

Liability-only coverage is cheaper — often in the $700–$1,300 annual range — but leaves your car unprotected and your personal assets exposed if you cause a serious crash.

The cheapest carrier for most Austin drivers depends on your profile. USAA is typically the least expensive but is only available to military members, veterans, and their families. Among carriers open to the general public, State Farm and Mercury frequently post the lowest average rates, followed by GEICO and Progressive. No single carrier is cheapest for everyone — that is exactly why comparing quotes matters.

## How to shop Austin auto insurance the right way

Most people shop auto insurance once, pick a price, and never look again. That is a mistake. Here is a better approach:

1. **Compare at least three carriers** at renewal, not just at initial purchase. Rates change, carriers enter and exit markets, and your driving record ages off over time.
2. **Match liability limits across quotes.** A $100,000/$300,000/$100,000 liability limit costs more than the 30/60/25 minimum but protects your assets far better. Compare apples to apples.
3. **Keep PIP and UM/UIM unless you have a specific reason to drop them.** Both are cheap relative to the protection they provide, especially in a metro with heavy uninsured-driver exposure.
4. **Set deductibles you can actually pay.** A $1,000 deductible lowers your premium but means $1,000 out of pocket after a hailstorm or fender bender. If that number would strain you, raise the deductible only on collision, not comprehensive.
5. **Bundle when it helps — but verify.** A multi-policy discount with the same carrier can save 10–20%, but only if the combined rate is actually lower than splitting auto and home across two well-priced carriers. Run the numbers.
6. **Ask about usage-based programs.** Several carriers offer telematics programs that track driving behavior and can discount safe drivers 10–30%. If you drive relatively few miles and avoid hard braking, it can be worth it.
