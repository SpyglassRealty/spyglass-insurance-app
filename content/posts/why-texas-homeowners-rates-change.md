If your homeowners renewal looks nothing like last year's premium, you are not imagining it. Texas rates move because of statewide weather losses, reinsurance costs, and the details of *your* property — not because a single bill arrived in the mail.

## Weather and catastrophe costs

Texas is exposed to hail, wind, wildfire, freeze events, and flooding. When carriers pay large numbers of claims in a region, they reprice the book of business. Hail in the I-35 corridor, winter storms that burst pipes, and severe thunderstorms all feed into the models insurers use to set rates.

Even if *you* did not file a claim, losses nearby can still influence your renewal. Reinsurance — the insurance companies buy for their own risk — has also become more expensive after several heavy catastrophe years, and that cost is passed through to policyholders.

## What is unique to your house

Carriers do not price every home the same. Common rating factors include:

- Roof age, material, and condition
- Year built and quality of electrical, plumbing, and HVAC updates
- Distance to fire protection and wildfire exposure
- Prior claims on the property or the insured
- Coverage A (dwelling) limit and replacement-cost estimates as rebuild costs rise
- Deductible choices, especially percentage deductibles for wind and hail
- Trampolines, pools, dogs, and other liability characteristics

Rebuild costs in Austin and surrounding counties have climbed with labor and materials. If your dwelling limit was not updated, a carrier may raise it at renewal — which raises premium even if the *rate* per $1,000 of coverage is unchanged.

## Market capacity and carrier appetite

Insurers enter and exit ZIP codes, construction types, and roof ages as their appetite changes. When fewer companies want a particular risk, remaining options can cost more. An independent agency's job is to watch that market and remarket the account when a renewal is no longer competitive — or when a carrier non-renews.

## What you can actually control

You cannot change the Texas hail season, but you can influence your outcome:

- Keep roof documentation and consider replacement when a roof is near the end of its insurable life
- Raise deductibles only if you can comfortably pay them after a storm
- Ask about wind/hail mitigation, water-leak sensors, and updated dwelling limits
- Avoid small claims that may cost more in future premiums than they return in payment
- Shop with an independent advisor at renewal, not only when a bill shocks you
